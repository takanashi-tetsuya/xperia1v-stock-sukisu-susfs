Changes
- fs/namei.c: remove the '..5.u.S' fallback entirely. lookup_dcache,
  lookup_one_qstr_excl, __lookup_slow and lookup_open reject a positive hidden
  inode with ENOENT, releasing their owned dentry exactly once. Creation and
  rename therefore do not receive either an unrelated sibling or a negative
  substitute dentry on the hidden pathname.
- lookup_fast: validate child/parent sequence counts and revalidate before the
  hidden decision. A positive hidden RCU lookup returns ECHILD to retry under
  ordinary reference ownership; it neither dputs the borrowed pointer nor calls
  d_lookup_done. Non-RCU lookup returns ENOENT after releasing its own reference.
  Ordinary revalidation errors, stale-cache retry, and allocation errors retain
  their normal handling.
- lookup_open: only the SUSFS-eligible app path performs a normal lookup before
  atomic_open, allowing the inode to be checked before an open can occur. A
  checked negative dentry can still use atomic_open and create the ORIGINAL name.
  Filesystem lookup aliases and errors retain normal release and lookup_done
  handling. Other callers retain the normal atomic-open path.
- fs/susfs.c: remove the unused global fake qstr name.
- fs/stat.c: regular and FUSE private kstat bits are now mutually exclusive;
  common mount-id assignment is shared.
- include/linux/susfs.h is byte-identical to the incoming 40900 source. ctime
  seconds remain bit 8 (KSTAT_SPOOF_CTIME_TV_SEC). Matching official CLI repair is
  owned by the parent task. Existing inode/device/FUSE predicates and readlink
  fixes are unchanged; their full original host regression passes.

Behavior and tradeoffs
A creation or rename aimed at an existing hidden inode returns ENOENT even when
its parent exists and is writable. This is deliberate fail-closed pathname
behavior: it never invents an alternative pathname. It does not offer a separate
virtual writable namespace at the hidden name. The former reserved-looking name
is once again just an ordinary file name when requested directly.
Eligible apps may incur an additional filesystem lookup before atomic_open.
This is necessary to inspect a cold positive inode before opening it. Tests do
not establish atomic hiding across remote-filesystem revalidation, policy
changes, or every possible atomic_open implementation. No claim of general
privilege bypass or all-filesystem/device correctness is made.
